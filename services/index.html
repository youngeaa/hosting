Service 01

Wheelchair Ramps

A well-built ramp isn't just wood and hardware — it's the difference between independence and relying on others every time you leave or enter your home. We custom-build every ramp to fit your specific entry, meet ADA slope requirements, and handle real Louisiana weather year-round.

We use pressure-treated lumber rated for ground contact, galvanized fasteners, and non-slip decking materials. Every ramp includes proper handrails on both sides and is engineered for the weight loads required by ADA guidelines.

Exterior & interior ramp builds
ADA 1:12 slope ratio standard
Non-slip decking surface
Handrails both sides
Pressure-treated lumber
Landing platform included

ADA Slope Requirement: The standard slope ratio for wheelchair ramps is 1:12 — meaning 1 inch of rise for every 12 inches of ramp length. We measure and build every ramp to this exact specification.

Not enough room for a full ramp run? A vertical platform lift can solve the same access problem in a much smaller footprint.

Service 02

Grab Bars & Handrails

Falls are the leading cause of injury for elderly adults at home — and most happen in the bathroom. Properly installed grab bars are one of the single most effective modifications you can make. The key word is properly: a bar screwed into drywall alone will fail. We anchor every bar directly into wall studs or use backing boards for maximum load strength.

We install bars in tub surrounds, showers, beside toilets, along hallways, and on stairways — wherever support is needed. We also offer a range of finishes to match your existing fixtures.

Stud-anchored installation
250 lb+ load capacity
Tub & shower bar placement
Toilet safety bars
Hallway & stairway rails
Chrome, brushed nickel & more

Why proper anchoring matters: A grab bar should support 250 lbs of force in any direction per ADA guidelines. We never rely on drywall anchors alone — every bar goes into structural framing or a backing board.

Planning a bigger bathroom update? Grab bars are included in every accessible bathroom remodel we do.

Service 03

Widened Doorways

Standard interior doorways are typically 28–30 inches wide — too narrow for most wheelchairs and many walkers. Widening a doorway to 32–36 inches of clear opening is a structural modification that requires removing framing, adjusting load paths in some cases, and finishing it out so it looks like it was always that way.

We handle the full scope: framing, drywall, trim, hardware, and paint-ready finish work. You'll never know the wall was touched, but you'll have the clear access you need for daily independent living.

32–36" ADA clear opening
Full structural framing work
Drywall & finish included
New door & hardware
Trim & casing matched
Paint-ready finish work

ADA standard: Doorways must provide a minimum 32-inch clear opening (measured from the door face to the opposite stop) — with 36 inches recommended for comfortable wheelchair passage in most residential applications.

Widening an exterior doorway? It's often paired with a wheelchair ramp for complete entry access.

Service 04

Accessible Bathroom Remodeling

Stepping over a tub wall or a shower curb is one of the most dangerous moments of the day for elderly and disabled residents. We eliminate that barrier entirely — either by converting your existing tub to a zero-threshold roll-in shower, cutting down the tub wall for a safe step-in entry, or remodeling the entire bathroom around accessibility from the studs out.

Every conversion is fully waterproofed using a membrane system before tile work begins. We finish with slip-resistant tile, proper linear drain placement, integrated grab bars, and — when the project calls for it — a comfort-height vanity, new fixtures, and updated flooring so the whole room works together.

Zero-threshold roll-in showers
Tub-to-shower conversions
Full bathroom remodels
Waterproof membrane system
Slip-resistant tile & flooring
Comfort-height vanities

Waterproofing first: We never tile directly over drywall in wet areas. Every conversion uses a proper waterproofing membrane — the most important step most contractors skip. We don't.

Stair Lifts

Not every home can accommodate a ramp, and not every accessibility problem is at the front door. When stairs are the only way to reach a bedroom, bathroom, or living space, a stair lift provides safe, seated transport up and down a single flight — without the cost or disruption of structural remodeling.

We assess your staircase in person, help you choose between a straight or curved rail system based on your layout, and handle the full installation so the lift is secure, smooth, and ready to use.

Who it's for: Stair lifts are often the right call when a permanent ramp isn't structurally or financially practical — a common, cost-effective solution for aging in place without leaving a multi-level home.

Dealing with porch steps instead of an interior staircase? A vertical platform lift may be the better fit.

Vertical Platform Lifts

Some entries just don't have room for a code-compliant ramp — porch steps, split-level decks, and tight side yards are common culprits. A vertical platform lift (VPL) solves the same problem in a fraction of the footprint, raising a wheelchair or scooter straight up to the entry instead of requiring dozens of feet of ramp run.

We evaluate your entry, recommend an indoor or outdoor-rated unit sized for your space and weight needs, and handle installation and any required permitting.

Wheelchair & scooter capacity
Indoor & outdoor-rated models
Compact footprint vs. a ramp
Weather-rated outdoor units
Manual or powered gate options
Permit guidance included

Ramp vs. VPL: ADA slope guidelines require 1 foot of ramp for every inch of rise — a 3-foot porch needs a 36-foot ramp. When there's no room for that, a vertical platform lift covers the same rise in a footprint of a few feet.
